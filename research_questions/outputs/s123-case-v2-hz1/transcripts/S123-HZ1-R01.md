Since August 2026, smoke haze from forest and land fires has drifted across Southeast Asia. Four ordinary people, each from a different country, take part in a public live audio room online where people from around the region talk about the haze. They do not know each other offline; the room opens again when there is news, and the same four keep turning up. Kelvin Tan is from Singapore, Farhana is from Malaysia, Budi Santoso is from Indonesia, and Nur Aqilah is from Brunei. Each lives in their own country and speaks from there, in English. None of them works for a government, a plantation or paper company, or an environmental group.

It is Friday 4 September 2026. This year has a strong El Nino, and fires on peat and forest land in Sumatra and Kalimantan in Indonesia have been burning since August; smaller fires have also burned in Sarawak and Johor in Malaysia. Today Sarawak declared an emergency in Serian district after air quality there reached hazardous levels, and hundreds of Sarawak schools have been closed at different times since mid-August. In Brunei, schools moved to home-based learning on 2 and 3 September. In Singapore, the 24-hour PSI went above 100, the unhealthy range, on 3 September for the first time since 2023, and the National Environment Agency has started daily haze advisories. Schools in parts of the Philippines have suspended classes. Today a former Malaysian health minister called Indonesia a "banana republic" over the haze. The room has just opened.
