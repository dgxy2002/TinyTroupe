## Opening situation

Four adults share a rented HDB flat with one kitchen. In the early evening, a persistent cooking aroma is noticeable in the kitchen and adjoining common area. The household has not established a rule about ventilation. Steady rain is blowing toward Casey's drying rack beside the closed kitchen window; opening it before the rack is moved could wet Casey's work clothes. The working extractor fan makes an audible hum in the common area. The shared utensils are already clean and personal utensils are visibly separated, so neither requires discussion.

A disagreement is already underway and everyone present heard it. Jamie told Alex to ventilate immediately and to accept a standing rule that whoever cooks must ventilate. Alex refused to accept a rule imposed during the incident and said the rain-facing window will stay closed while Casey's laundry is exposed; if Jamie wants the window opened, Jamie should arrange to move the drying rack. Jamie replied that managing cooking odour is the cook's responsibility and should not be shifted to another flatmate. Casey has not consented to anyone moving the rack. Robin then asked Alex to release the only stove within two minutes, but Alex said the meal still needed about ten minutes. Casey is standing beside the drying rack. The ventilation responsibility, future rule, and stove-access demands are all unsettled, and no existing household rule determines whose claim has priority.
