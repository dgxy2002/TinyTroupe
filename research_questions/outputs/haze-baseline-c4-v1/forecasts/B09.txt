The forecast assumes no major, unexpected weather event, such as the sudden arrival of a tropical storm, that would douse the fires. The El Niño conditions will persist.

### Indonesia

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **Damage Control (14-16 October):** The Indonesian government will move to control the diplomatic fallout from Syahganda Nainggolan's "oxygen" comment. Foreign Minister Retno Marsudi will issue a statement within the next two days clarifying that Mr. Nainggolan's remarks do not represent the official government position. She will state that Indonesia takes the haze seriously and values its relationship with its neighbours. President Prabowo Subianto will not comment directly, letting his minister handle it, to maintain his presidential stature above the fray.
*   **Symbolic Acceptance of Aid (17-19 October):** To placate Malaysia and appear cooperative, Indonesia will finally respond to Malaysia’s offer of assistance. However, echoing their disaster agency's preference, they will accept the offer of 52 ground personnel but politely decline the aircraft, citing "sufficient aerial assets already deployed". This will be a face-saving measure.
*   **Enforcement Actions (by 22 October):** To demonstrate the seriousness of President Prabowo's "maximum effort" order, the Indonesian National Police will announce the arrest of at least one mid-level plantation manager from a foreign-linked company. They will publicize the name of the company and the individual, and hold a press conference with seized evidence (e.g., maps, jerrycans of fuel) to show tangible results.

**Ordinary People's Reactions:**

*   **In Sumatra and Kalimantan:** Residents will grow more desperate. Local citizen journalists will post videos of low visibility, respiratory clinics overwhelmed with patients, and small, spontaneous protests outside local government offices demanding more masks and immediate healthcare assistance. The hashtag #KamiButuhUdaraBersih (We Need Clean Air) will trend locally.
*   **In Jakarta and other major cities:** The "oxygen" comment will become a viral source of dark humour and memes among urban, educated Indonesians, who will express embarrassment on social media. However, a significant nationalist segment will defend the comment, creating heated online debates. General awareness of the haze's severity will increase, but without direct physical impact, it will not be the primary public concern.

### Malaysia

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **Diplomatic Protest (14-15 October):** Foreign Minister Zambry Abdul Kadir will summon the Indonesian ambassador to Malaysia to express a formal protest over Syahganda Nainggolan's "unhelpful and insensitive" remarks. The Malaysian government will issue a strongly-worded press statement afterwards, making the summons public.
*   **Targeting Companies (16-20 October):** Picking up on the MP's call, Minister for Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Nik Nazmi Nik Ahmad will announce that the government is initiating a formal investigation into the Malaysian-linked companies named in the Greenpeace report. He will not threaten immediate license revocations but will state that "all economic and legal options are on the table," including engaging with Malaysian banks that finance these entities.
*   **Domestic Measures (Ongoing):** The school closures in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, and other affected states will be extended until at least Friday, 23 October. The Minister of Health, Dr. Dzulkefly Ahmad, will hold a press conference advising the public, especially the elderly and children, to stay indoors and announcing the distribution of N95 masks at government clinics in the worst-hit areas.

**Ordinary People's Reactions:**

*   **Public Outrage and Boycott:** The "oxygen" comment will galvanize public anger. The Malaysian Consumers Association (FOMCA) will issue a statement supporting a consumer boycott of products from companies directly linked to the fires. Lists of these brands (e.g., specific cooking oils, paper products) will circulate widely on WhatsApp and Facebook, with a noticeable, though temporary, drop in their sales. The hashtag #HazeIsNotOxygen will trend nationally.
*   **Civil Society Action:** A coalition of environmental NGOs will organize a peaceful protest of about 200-300 people outside the Indonesian Embassy in Kuala Lumpur on Friday, 16 October, demanding accountability. Protesters will wear N95 masks and carry signs saying "Your business = Our sickness."

### Singapore

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **Measured Diplomatic Response (14-15 October):** Minister for Sustainability and the Environment Grace Fu will issue a public statement expressing "grave concern" over the worsening haze and noting that Mr. Nainggolan's comments were "unconstructive to the collaborative spirit needed to resolve this regional issue." The statement will reiterate Singapore's standing offer to help Indonesia, including a C-130 aircraft for cloud seeding operations.
*   **Enhanced Public Health Advisories (by 16 October):** As the 24-hour PSI threatens to cross the 200 "Very Unhealthy" mark, the National Environment Agency (NEA) will upgrade its daily advisory. It will strongly advise all citizens to minimise outdoor activity and for vulnerable persons to not go out at all. The Ministry of Education will announce that if the PSI enters the "Hazardous" range (above 300), schools will immediately switch to home-based learning.
*   **Corporate Pressure (19-22 October):** The Singapore Exchange (SGX) will issue a "Trade with Caution" advisory on the Singapore-listed companies named in the Greenpeace report, citing "reputational and environmental risks that may impact shareholder value." This is a significant step that will put direct market pressure on the companies.

**Ordinary People's Reactions:**

*   **Surging Demand and "Haze Fatigue":** Sales of high-quality N95 masks (especially 3M brands) and powerful air purifiers (like Dyson and Philips) will spike, with some retailers running out of stock. There will be widespread public anger expressed online, particularly on Reddit's r/singapore and HardwareZone forums, with much of the vitriol directed at both the Indonesian government and the implicated Singaporean companies.
*   **Scrutiny and Grumbling:** There will be a post-mortem on the decision to hold the F1 race. Op-eds will appear in The Straits Times and on CNA's website from commentators questioning the wisdom of proceeding with the event and the potential long-term health impact on attendees.

### Brunei

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **Quiet Diplomacy and Public Health (Ongoing):** The government will not issue any strong public statements against Indonesia, preferring to work through ASEAN channels. The Ministry of Health will continue to issue daily public service announcements via Radio Televisyen Brunei, advising residents to limit time outdoors and wear masks.
*   **Extended School Measures (by 15 October):** The Ministry of Education will announce that the current period of home-based learning will be extended for at least another week, until 23 October, given that air quality shows no sign of improvement.

**Ordinary People's Reactions:**

*   **Resignation and Adaptation:** The public mood will be one of frustrated resignation. Social media feeds will be dominated by photos of the hazy, sepia-toned sky over landmarks like the Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque. Families will cancel weekend plans, and businesses that rely on foot traffic, like cafes and outdoor markets, will report a sharp decline in customers. The reaction will be one of adaptation and complaint rather than organised protest.
