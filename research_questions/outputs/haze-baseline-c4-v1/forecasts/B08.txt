Based on the news updates, here is a forecast for the next ten days, from Wednesday 14 October to Friday 23 October 2026.

The environmental situation will not improve. The strong El Niño means no significant rain is likely to fall over Sumatra and Kalimantan. Prevailing winds will continue to blow smoke north and east, meaning air quality in Malaysia and Singapore will remain in the 'Unhealthy' to 'Very Unhealthy' range, with a high probability of reaching 'Hazardous' levels for short periods.

### **Indonesia**

*   **Government:**
    *   **Damage Control (14-16 Oct):** Stung by the international ridicule from Syahganda Nainggolan's "oxygen" comment, Indonesia's Foreign Minister, Retno Marsudi, will issue a diplomatic statement. She will not apologize but will state that Mr. Nainggolan's remarks "do not reflect the government's official position" and will stress that President Prabowo is treating the fires with the "utmost seriousness."
    *   **Visible Action (17-19 Oct):** To demonstrate his "maximum efforts" order, President Prabowo Subianto will conduct a highly publicized visit to a fire-stricken area in Central Kalimantan, accompanied by the head of the disaster agency (BNPB). He will be photographed talking to firefighters and will announce the deployment of an additional 1,000 soldiers to help with firefighting efforts on the ground.
    *   **Enforcement Announcement (20-22 Oct):** The Indonesian National Police will announce a significant development in their investigation, naming and formally charging two mid-level plantation managers (one from a Malaysian-linked company on the Greenpeace list) for negligence. This will be presented as proof that Indonesia is taking action against the perpetrators.
    *   **Diplomacy:** Indonesia will continue to publicly decline offers of aircraft, but will quietly accept technical assistance from Japan in the form of satellite data analysis to identify new hotspots. They will not formally respond to Malaysia's offer of help.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   In haze-affected cities like Pekanbaru and Palangkaraya, respiratory illnesses will rise sharply. Local media will feature stories of overflowing clinics and children on nebulizers. Citizen-led groups will organize mask distribution drives.
    *   On Indonesian social media, particularly Twitter/X, there will be a defensive, nationalistic reaction to the anger from Malaysia and Singapore. The hashtag #TerimaKasihOksigen ("Thank you for the oxygen") will be used sarcastically by some to mock their neighbours, while others will express shame and anger at their own government's handling of the crisis and the embarrassing official comments.

### **Malaysia**

*   **Government:**
    *   **Diplomatic Protest (14-15 Oct):** Foreign Minister Zambry Abdul Kadir will summon the Indonesian Ambassador to Malaysia to Wisma Putra to hand over a diplomatic note. The note will formally protest the "unacceptable" remarks by the Indonesian official and express "deep disappointment" over the lack of response to Malaysia's offer of assistance made nearly a month ago.
    *   **Economic Pressure (16-20 Oct):** The MP's call for a boycott will gain political traction. While the government will not officially endorse a boycott, the Minister of Domestic Trade and Costs of Living, Armizan Mohd Ali, will state in Parliament that the government "supports Malaysian consumers in making informed choices" and will order a review of supply chains for major supermarket chains to identify products from companies on the Greenpeace list.
    *   **Further Closures (by 19 Oct):** As air quality worsens, the Ministry of Education will extend the school closures to the entire states of Selangor, Melaka, and Negeri Sembilan for the rest of the week. Another 1,500 schools will be affected.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   The boycott call will go viral. A social media campaign, with the hashtag #HazeBoycott, will circulate lists of consumer products (instant noodles, cooking oil, biscuits) produced by the companies named by Greenpeace. Supermarket shelves with these items will be photographed and shared online.
    *   Public anger will be directed at Indonesia. The Facebook and Instagram pages of the Indonesian Embassy in Kuala Lumpur will be flooded with thousands of angry comments from Malaysians.
    *   Sales of N95 masks and air purifiers will surge. Pharmacists will report shortages of masks in Kuala Lumpur and Johor Bahru.

### **Singapore**

*   **Government:**
    *   **Public Health Response (14-16 Oct):** With the PSI forecast to enter the 'Very Unhealthy' range (above 200), Minister for Sustainability and the Environment Grace Fu will hold a press conference. She will announce that N95 masks will be made available for collection by vulnerable and lower-income households at community centres. She will strongly advise all companies to implement work-from-home policies where possible.
    *   **Corporate Accountability (17-20 Oct):** Under public pressure, the National Environment Agency (NEA) will issue a formal statement confirming it has served notices under the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act to the Singapore-based companies named by Greenpeace, requiring them to provide information on their operations in Indonesia.
    *   **Sporting Event Contingency (by 22 Oct):** With a major international marathon scheduled for the following weekend, organizers will announce a contingency plan, stating the event will be cancelled if the 24-hour PSI remains above 200 on race day.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   The "oxygen" remark will be relentlessly mocked. Witty and sarcastic memes will circulate widely on WhatsApp and Instagram, with one popular format showing a person in an N95 mask with the caption: "Returning your oxygen, Indonesia. Keep it."
    *   There will be a notable drop in foot traffic in outdoor dining areas and shopping districts like Orchard Road. Food delivery services like GrabFood and Foodpanda will see a significant spike in orders.
    *   Online forums like HardwareZone and Reddit will be dominated by threads complaining about the government's perceived "soft" diplomatic approach and sharing DIY methods for sealing windows against the haze.

### **Brunei**

*   **Government:**
    *   **Precautionary Measures (by 16 Oct):** As the haze from Sarawak and Kalimantan drifts over the country, the Ministry of Health will issue an advisory urging the public, especially the elderly and those with respiratory conditions, to stay indoors.
    *   **School Closures (by 19 Oct):** The Ministry of Education will announce the closure of all primary and secondary schools nationwide for at least three days as the Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) in all districts climbs into the unhealthy range.
    *   **Diplomatic Stance:** The government will make no public statements directed at Indonesia or Malaysia, preferring to work through ASEAN's private channels.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   The public mood will be one of frustrated resignation. People will cancel outdoor plans, such as visits to Tasek Lama Recreational Park.
    *   Social media activity will focus on sharing PSI readings from the government app and photos of the hazy sky, particularly the obscured view of the Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque. There will be widespread sharing of Malaysian news reports about the diplomatic spat with Indonesia.
