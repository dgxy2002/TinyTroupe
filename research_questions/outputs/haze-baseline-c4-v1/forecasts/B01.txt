The forecast assumes that the strong El Niño weather pattern will continue, meaning there will be no significant rainfall to douse the fires. The haze situation is therefore expected to persist and likely worsen before any potential improvement.

### **Overall Situation**

The diplomatic fallout from the Indonesian official's "we send oxygen" remark will dominate the next 3-4 days, escalating tensions particularly with Malaysia. Public anger will coalesce around specific targets—the companies named by Greenpeace—leading to concrete consumer action. With air quality remaining in the unhealthy to hazardous range, governments will be forced to take more visible action, both domestically and diplomatically.

### **Indonesia**

*   **Government:**
    *   **By Thursday, 15 October:** President Prabowo Subianto's administration will engage in damage control. The Foreign Minister, Retno Marsudi, will issue a statement attempting to de-escalate the "oxygen" comment, likely stating it was the personal opinion of one official and does not reflect Indonesia's official stance of valuing its neighbours. There will be no formal apology.
    *   **By Friday, 16 October:** To demonstrate the "maximum efforts" ordered by the President, the Indonesian National Police will announce the arrest of several more plantation managers and the suspension of the license of one of the 95 companies under investigation. This will be timed to be the lead story on the Friday evening news.
    *   **Early next week (approx. 19-20 October):** Faced with continued international pressure, Indonesia will quietly accept "technical assistance" from Japan or another neutral party (like satellite imagery analysis or weather modelling), while continuing to publicly decline offers of personnel and equipment from Malaysia and Singapore, framing it as an issue of national sovereignty and operational preference.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   **In Sumatra and Kalimantan:** Residents will continue to suffer. Local news outlets will feature stories of overflowing clinics and a rise in respiratory illnesses. Citizen journalists will post videos of the thick, yellow smoke and near-zero visibility, directly contradicting the defensive government statements.
    *   **In Jakarta and online:** The "oxygen" comment will have a mixed reaction. A significant nationalist segment on social media platforms like X (formerly Twitter) and TikTok will rally behind Syahganda Nainggolan, creating memes that mock Singapore and Malaysia for being dependent on Indonesian forests. This will fuel online arguments with Malaysian and Singaporean netizens.

### **Malaysia**

*   **Government:**
    *   **Wednesday, 14 October:** The Foreign Minister, Zambry Abdul Kadir, will formally summon the Indonesian ambassador to Wisma Putra to lodge a protest over the "oxygen" remark and the lack of response to Malaysia's offer of aid.
    *   **By Friday, 16 October:** The call for boycotts by the MP will gain traction. The Minister of Domestic Trade and Cost of Living, Armizan Ali, will issue a statement saying consumers have the right to choose, effectively giving a tacit government endorsement to the boycott movement.
    *   **By Tuesday, 20 October:** Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim will use an upcoming ASEAN-related meeting or press conference to make a much stronger statement, urging the ASEAN Secretariat to implement more robust enforcement mechanisms for the Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   **Immediately:** The boycott call will go viral. Consumer groups and influencers will create and circulate "haze-free" shopping lists, specifically naming and shaming brands owned by the companies on the Greenpeace list. Expect to see photos on social media of supermarket shelves with boycotted products (e.g., certain brands of cooking oil, paper, and instant noodles) left untouched. #BoikotJerebu (#BoycottHaze) will become a trending hashtag.
    *   **Throughout the period:** There will be a surge in demand for N95 masks and air purifiers, with pharmacies in the Klang Valley, Melaka and Johor reporting shortages by the weekend. More event cancellations will be announced, including outdoor sporting events and weekend markets. Anger will be directed not just at Indonesia, but also at Malaysian-linked companies implicated in the fires.

### **Singapore**

*   **Government:**
    *   **By Thursday, 15 October:** The Minister for Sustainability and the Environment, Grace Fu, will hold a multi-ministry press conference. She will express "deep disappointment" with Indonesia's response and state that the National Environment Agency (NEA) has served legal notices on several Singapore-linked companies on the Greenpeace list, requiring them to provide information on their operations in Indonesia, as a first step under the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act.
    *   **Over the weekend (17-18 October):** As the 24-hour PSI likely breaches the 200 "Very Unhealthy" level, the Ministry of Education will announce that if conditions do not improve, schools will move to home-based learning for a few days starting from Monday, 19 October. The Ministry of Health will strongly advise all companies to allow employees to work from home where possible.
    *   **Throughout the period:** The government will begin distributing N95 masks to vulnerable and lower-income households through community centres.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   Discontent will grow, but the reaction will be more pragmatic than in Malaysia. The primary focus will be on self-preservation. Sales of air purifiers, N95 masks, and even Manuka honey (a popular folk remedy for sore throats) will spike.
    *   On social media, Singaporeans will share photos of the hazy skyline and screenshots of their PSI readings. The tone will be one of weary frustration, with comments like "Here we go again" and "When will they ever learn?" There will be less focus on boycotts and more on criticising the perceived ineffectiveness of ASEAN and their own government's diplomatic efforts.

### **Brunei**

*   **Government:**
    *   **By Friday, 16 October:** The Ministry of Education will announce an extension of home-based learning for all schools for another week.
    *   **Diplomatically:** Brunei's government will remain publicly silent on the Indonesia-Malaysia spat. However, behind the scenes, its diplomats will express their concerns directly to their Indonesian counterparts through ASEAN channels, emphasizing the health impacts and disruption. No public condemnation will be made.

*   **Ordinary People:**
    *   Life will be significantly disrupted. Parents will complain on Facebook groups about the difficulty of juggling work and supervising home-based learning. There will be a general sense of resignation and acceptance, as Brunei is often a downwind recipient of the haze. Most public discussion will revolve around sharing health tips and complaining about the cancellation of outdoor activities.
