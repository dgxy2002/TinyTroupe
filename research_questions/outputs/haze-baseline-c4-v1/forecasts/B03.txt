*   **14-16 October:** The Indonesian government will go into damage control over Syahganda Nainggolan's "we send oxygen" remark. Foreign Minister Retno Marsudi or Environment Minister Siti Nurbaya Bakar will issue a statement clarifying that his comments do not represent the official government position. They will emphasize that Indonesia takes the fires seriously and is the biggest victim. However, they will still not formally accept Malaysia's offer of assistance, with a spokesperson for the disaster agency (BNPB) stating that Indonesian ground personnel are more effective for the current conditions.
*   **17-19 October:** To demonstrate action following President Prabowo's order, the National Police will announce the arrest of 15-20 more individuals, mostly smallholders and a few mid-level plantation managers. They will also publicise the sealing of land belonging to 3-4 more companies. President Prabowo will be photographed visiting the national disaster control room in Jakarta or flying over a fire-affected area in Kalimantan to project an image of command.
*   **20-23 October:** Facing sustained diplomatic pressure, Indonesia’s Foreign Ministry will issue a formal statement calling for "regional unity" and warning against "counter-productive actions" (a veiled reference to the boycott calls in Malaysia). President Prabowo Subianto may have another, more terse, phone call with Malaysian PM Anwar Ibrahim, where he will promise continued action but ask for "patience and understanding."

**Public Reaction:**

*   Nationalist sentiment will be stoked by the Malaysian criticism. On social media platforms like X (formerly Twitter) and TikTok, many Indonesians will rally behind Syahganda Nainggolan's remarks, framing them as a necessary defence of national pride. The hashtag #BelaIndonesia (#DefendIndonesia) will trend.
*   Conversely, environmentally-conscious citizens and those in fire-affected provinces like Riau and Central Kalimantan will express their anger online, sharing photos of the thick haze and accusing their own government of incompetence and corruption. Activist groups like WALHI (The Indonesian Forum for Environment) will hold small, symbolic protests outside the Ministry of Environment and Forestry in Jakarta.

### **Malaysia**

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **14-16 October:** The call for boycotts by the MP will dominate the political discourse. Minister of Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability, Nik Nazmi Nik Ahmad, will state that while the government does not officially endorse a public boycott, it understands the people's "deep frustration." He will publicly state for the third time that Malaysia's offer of firefighting assistance remains on the table and that he is disappointed by the lack of a formal response. School closures in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, and other affected states will be extended until at least Friday, 16 October.
*   **17-20 October:** Frustration will boil over into formal diplomatic action. Foreign Minister Zambry Abdul Kadir will summon the Indonesian Ambassador to Malaysia to Wisma Putra (the Foreign Ministry) to hand over a diplomatic note of protest, formally expressing Malaysia's displeasure with the transboundary haze and the inflammatory "oxygen" comment.
*   **21-23 October:** Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim will make a stern public statement after a cabinet meeting, declaring that the haze is "testing the limits of ASEAN solidarity" and that Malaysia is exploring legal avenues under the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution.

**Public Reaction:**

*   The boycott call will gain significant traction. Consumer groups will publish lists of products produced by the companies named in the Greenpeace report. On Facebook and Instagram, a grassroots campaign will emerge with the hashtag #HazeFreeProducts, where users post pictures of alternative brands they are buying. Major supermarket chains like Jaya Grocer or Village Grocer will feel public pressure and may issue statements that they are "reviewing their sourcing policies."
*   Ordinary Malaysians will be highly vocal about the disruption. Parents' groups on WhatsApp will be flooded with complaints about the extended school closures and concerns over their children's health. Sales of N95 masks and air purifiers will surge, with pharmacies in the Klang Valley reporting stock shortages.

### **Singapore**

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **14-16 October:** The Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment (MSE) will issue a strongly-worded statement expressing "grave concern" over the worsening haze. It will announce that the National Environment Agency (NEA) has formally written to its Indonesian counterpart to request the latest fire hotspot data and has also contacted the Singaporean-linked companies named in the Greenpeace report, demanding they share information on their fire-prevention measures in their Indonesian concessions.
*   **18-20 October:** With the 24-hour PSI forecast to remain in the "Unhealthy" or "Very Unhealthy" range, the government will announce the distribution of N95 masks from the national stockpile to vulnerable and lower-income households via community centres. The Ministry of Education will announce that if the PSI enters the "Hazardous" range (above 300), schools will immediately switch to home-based learning.
*   **21-23 October:** The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) will issue a statement reminding financial institutions of their obligation to consider environmental risks in their lending and investment decisions, a clear signal to the banks financing the implicated companies. The Singapore Exchange (SGX) will also formally query the listed companies named in the report, requiring them to publicly disclose their actions regarding the fires.

**Public Reaction:**

*   Singaporeans will react with a mixture of anger and resignation. The "haze-gram" will become common on Instagram, with people posting pictures of the skyline obscured by haze. There will be widespread online criticism of the Indonesian government, particularly the "oxygen" comment, which will be seen as an arrogant dismissal of Singaporeans' suffering.
*   There will be a strong "buy local" push, but less of an overt boycott movement than in Malaysia. Instead, the pressure will be directed at their own government and corporations to "do something." Online forums like HardwareZone and Reddit will have threads tracking the PSI minute-by-minute and debating the effectiveness of different air purifier brands.

### **Brunei**

**Government Actions & Statements:**

*   **14-17 October:** The Ministry of Health will issue daily public service announcements, advising citizens, especially children and the elderly, to limit outdoor activities. The Department of Environment, Parks and Recreation (JASTRe) will continue to publish daily Air Pollutant Index (API) readings from its four monitoring stations.
*   **18-23 October:** Brunei's government will likely work through ASEAN channels. The Minister of Foreign Affairs II, Erywan Yusof, will likely coordinate with his Malaysian and Singaporean counterparts. Brunei will co-sign any joint statement issued through the ASEAN Secretariat calling for adherence to the transboundary haze agreement. They will not issue a strong unilateral condemnation of Indonesia.

**Public Reaction:**

*   Bruneians will express their concerns on social media, sharing pictures of the hazy conditions in Bandar Seri Begawan and Kuala Belait. Similar to their neighbours, there will be an increase in the purchase of face masks. The public reaction will be one of frustrated concern rather than the outright public anger seen in Malaysia and Singapore, reflecting the nation's more reserved public sphere.
