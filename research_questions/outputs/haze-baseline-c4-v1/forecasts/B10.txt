Based on the news updates, here is a forecast for the next ten days, from Wednesday, 14 October to Friday, 23 October 2026.

### Executive Summary

The situation will escalate diplomatically and socially before it improves environmentally. The inflammatory "oxygen" remark from an Indonesian official will trigger a sharp response from Malaysia and public anger across the region. This will force Indonesia into a damage-control mode, leading to more visible (but not necessarily more effective) firefighting efforts. Citizen-led economic pressure, such as boycotts, will gain significant traction in Malaysia and Singapore, targeting companies named in the Greenpeace report. The haze itself will likely persist or worsen for several days before any potential improvement from weather changes or firefighting efforts.

### **Indonesia**

**Government:**
*   **14-15 October:** Indonesia's Foreign Ministry, under Minister Retno Marsudi, will issue a clarifying statement to "walk back" Syahganda Nainggolan's "oxygen" comment. The statement will not be an apology but will stress that his words do not represent the government's official position. It will reaffirm Indonesia's commitment to ASEAN solidarity and its ongoing efforts to fight the fires.
*   **16-18 October:** In response to President Prabowo's order for "maximum efforts", the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB) will hold a high-profile press conference. They will announce the deployment of an additional 2,000 military and police personnel to Riau and Central Kalimantan and publicize the number of water-bombing sorties flown by Indonesian aircraft. They will also announce the arrest of 15-20 more individuals, including a mid-level manager from one of the investigated plantation companies.
*   **19-21 October:** Facing continued pressure, President Prabowo Subianto will make a direct public address. He will state that Indonesia is using all its internal resources to fight the fires but will now formally invite "technical experts" from Malaysia and Singapore to share best practices on peatland fire management, pointedly avoiding the acceptance of aircraft or personnel. This is a face-saving gesture.

**Ordinary People:**
*   Indonesians in Jakarta will be largely split. Nationalist commentators and social media accounts will defend Nainggolan's statement, creating viral memes and engaging in online flame wars with Malaysians under hashtags like `#TerimaKasihOksigen` (Thank you for the oxygen).
*   However, citizens in haze-choked cities like Pekanbaru and Palangkaraya will express extreme frustration online, posting videos of the thick yellow smog and stories of children with respiratory infections. Local environmental NGOs in Kalimantan will directly condemn the government's slow response and the "oxygen" comment as an embarrassment.

### **Malaysia**

**Government:**
*   **14 October:** Foreign Minister Zambry Abdul Kadir will summon the Indonesian Ambassador to Malaysia to Wisma Putra to hand over a diplomatic note protesting the "unhelpful and insensitive" remarks by the Indonesian official.
*   **15-16 October:** Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim will make another public statement, this time with a sterner tone. He will announce that he has instructed the Ministry of Plantation and Commodities to begin an immediate audit of the Malaysian companies named in the Greenpeace report, and warns of penalties, including the potential suspension of operating licenses abroad.
*   **19-22 October:** With air quality remaining in the "Very Unhealthy" range, the Minister of Education, Fadhlina Sidek, will extend the school closures in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, and Putrajaya until at least 23 October. The government will also likely declare a state of emergency in Muar or another badly affected district in Johor.

**Ordinary People:**
*   The call from the Malaysian MP to boycott will be acted upon immediately. Consumer groups and social media influencers will create and circulate graphics listing specific cooking oil, soap, and snack food brands owned by the companies on the Greenpeace list. Supermarket chains like Mydin may see small, spontaneous protests.
*   The hashtag `#BoikotJerebu` (Boycott Haze) will trend nationally. Malaysians will share photos of their empty N95 mask boxes and sold-out shelves in pharmacies. There will be a surge in purchases of indoor air purifiers, with major brands selling out online. Frustration with both Indonesia's government and complicit Malaysian corporations will merge into a single wave of anger.

### **Singapore**

**Government:**
*   **14-16 October:** The Ministry of Foreign Affairs will issue a carefully worded statement expressing "grave concern" over the transboundary haze and the "unfortunate comments" from Jakarta. It will publicly reiterate its standing offer of a C-130 aircraft, a team of firefighters, and high-resolution satellite imagery to the Indonesian government.
*   **17-19 October:** As the 24-hour PSI breaches the 200 "Very Unhealthy" level, Minister for Sustainability and the Environment Grace Fu will hold a press conference. She will announce that major outdoor sporting and community events scheduled for the upcoming weekend are to be cancelled or postponed. The government will also activate a plan to distribute N95 masks to the 1-2 million households in its national stockpile.
*   **20-23 October:** The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) will issue a directive "reminding" financial institutions in Singapore to review their exposure to and financing of companies implicated in unsustainable burning practices, signalling a more serious threat of financial action.

**Ordinary People:**
*   There will be widespread public anger, channelled through platforms like Reddit and Facebook. The "oxygen" comment will be mercilessly mocked. Singaporeans will express frustration that the haze has returned with such severity despite years of diplomatic efforts.
*   "Panic buying" of N95 masks and air purifiers will intensify. Retailers like FairPrice and Watsons will run out of stock. Parents will form online groups to share tips on home-based activities for children as they are urged to keep them indoors. The decision to run the F1 Grand Prix the previous weekend will be heavily criticized in hindsight in letters to the Straits Times editor.

### **Brunei**

**Government:**
*   **14-16 October:** The Ministry of Health will elevate its public health advisory, urging all citizens, not just the vulnerable, to avoid strenuous outdoor activity. The Ministry of Education will announce that home-based learning will continue for all schools until at least Friday, 23 October.
*   **17-20 October:** Brunei's government will primarily work through ASEAN channels. The Minister of Foreign Affairs II, Erywan Yusof, will likely confer with his Malaysian and Singaporean counterparts and support a joint ASEAN ministerial statement on the haze, urging for stronger collective action.

**Ordinary People:**
*   The mood will be one of fatigue and resignation. Social media will be filled with photos of the hazy sky over the Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque. Bruneians will mostly focus on practical coping mechanisms, with a noticeable drop in traffic and patronage at outdoor food stalls. There will be less of the overt anger seen in Malaysia and Singapore, and more quiet frustration and concern over health impacts.
