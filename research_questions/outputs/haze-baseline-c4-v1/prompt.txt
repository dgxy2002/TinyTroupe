It is Tuesday 13 October 2026. Below is the news about the Southeast Asian haze so far this year, in order. Based on it, forecast what will happen over the next ten days: what the governments of Indonesia, Singapore, Malaysia and Brunei will do or say, and how ordinary people in each country will react. Give concrete, specific predictions (who does what), not general statements.

News update 1:
It is Friday 4 September 2026. This year has a strong El Nino, and fires on peat and forest land in Sumatra and Kalimantan in Indonesia have been burning since August; smaller fires have also burned in Sarawak and Johor in Malaysia. Today Sarawak declared an emergency in Serian district after air quality there reached hazardous levels, and hundreds of Sarawak schools have been closed at different times since mid-August. In Brunei, schools moved to home-based learning on 2 and 3 September. In Singapore, the 24-hour PSI went above 100, the unhealthy range, on 3 September for the first time since 2023, and the National Environment Agency has started daily haze advisories. Schools in parts of the Philippines have suspended classes. Today a former Malaysian health minister called Indonesia a "banana republic" over the haze.

News update 2:
It is now Friday 18 September 2026. Air quality is unhealthy at half of Malaysia's 68 monitoring stations, and the haze reached southern Vietnam on 14 September. Malaysia has offered Indonesia one firefighting plane, three helicopters and 52 personnel. Japan has sent military helicopters to help Indonesia fight the fires. Indonesian police say 95 companies are being investigated over the fires, along with many individuals.

News update 3:
It is now Friday 9 October 2026. On 7 October Greenpeace Malaysia published a list of the 20 entities most linked to this year's fires in Indonesia; it includes Malaysian and Singaporean companies. All schools in Johor have been closed since 7 October, and today schools were also ordered closed in Melaka, Negeri Sembilan, Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya. In Singapore the 24-hour PSI reached 192 this evening, the highest this year, on the weekend of the Formula One Grand Prix, which is going ahead. Malaysia's Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said he had called Indonesia's President Prabowo Subianto, who agreed to take the necessary steps.

News update 4:
It is now Tuesday 13 October 2026. On 10 October the head of Indonesia's Government Communications Agency, Syahganda Nainggolan, said the fires were "beyond human error; this is truly nature" and added: "We're not just sending over the haze. When there's no haze, we send oxygen. The oxygen from Singapore and Malaysia is from our forests, right?" A video of the remark is being widely shared. Indonesia's disaster agency said ground personnel are preferred to aircraft from neighbours. On 11 October Malaysia said it had still had no reply to its offer of help. On 12 October President Prabowo ordered maximum efforts against the fires. Today a Malaysian MP told Parliament that Malaysians should boycott companies linked to the burning ("We need to hit them where they hurt the most") and that banks should stop financing them. Malaysia's Firefly airline has cancelled flights to Singapore because of the haze.
