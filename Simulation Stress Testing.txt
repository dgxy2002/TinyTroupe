During your Week 1 stress testing with Candidate 1 (Hawker Centre "This Table Is Choped"), your goal is to evaluate TinyTroupe's operational boundaries, stability, and persona fidelity across 5–10 repeated runs before conducting full scenario experiments.
To give your advisor a complete, scientifically defensible evaluation, you should track four categories of metrics:
1. Framework Stability & Convergence Metrics
These metrics test whether the simulation produces stable, repeatable social outcomes across independent runs or diverges into random noise.
* Macro-Outcome Distribution: Classify the final resolution of each run into one of four mutually exclusive categories: Consensus (all agents accept the norm), Compromise (a practical alternative is negotiated), Avoidance / Indirect Resolution (an agent yields without explicit resolution), or Escalation (disagreement intensifies). Track whether this distribution stabilizes across 5–10 repeated runs.
* Turns-to-Resolution (Interaction Horizon): Count the number of dialogue turns required for agents to reach an outcome or issue the DONE action 1.
* Trajectory Divergence & Dispersion: Calculate the semantic dispersion (e.g., Pairwise Cosine Distance of conversation embeddings across runs) to verify whether the global outcome stabilizes across runs while preserving local dialogue variation 2, 3.
2. Persona Adherence & Cultural Fidelity Metrics
These metrics measure how well agents stay in character without drifting into generic, overly polite AI assistants.
* Persona Adherence Score: Utilize TinyTroupe's Propositions (which score adherence on a scale from 0 to 9 via LLM-as-a-Judge) to evaluate if actions directly match the agent's specified background (e.g., Mr. Lim's age/language profile vs. Rachel's overseas background) 4-6.
* Profile Faithfulness & Self-Consistency: Monitor whether agents remain self-consistent over time and adhere to expected demographic behaviors (e.g., age deference toward senior citizens) 6, 7.
* Norm Recognition & Violation Rate: Track whether agents recognize the tissue packet as a chope reservation norm and whether the target agent violates or respects the location-conditioned norm 7.
3. Behavioral Integrity & Multi-Agent Dynamics
These metrics monitor whether multi-agent interaction maintains diverse persona perspectives or suffers from structural failure modes.
* Sycophancy & Diversity Collapse Rate: Track whether agents immediately cave into premature consensus on Turn 1 (a classic failure mode in multi-agent debate) or engage in multi-turn negotiation and stance defense 8-10.
* Constructive Disagreement & Friction: Measure the ratio of turns where agents express genuine disagreement or offer explanations rather than immediate, superficial agreement 11, 12.
* Linguistic & Communication Markers:
* Code-Switching & Singlish Frequency: Count local particles (lah, leh, lor, uncle, auntie, Hokkien/Mandarin/Malay phrases) across agents.
* Direct vs. Indirect Communication: Distinguish between direct statements ("I reserved this table") and face-saving indirect language ("Actually, I think this table got people already").
* Politeness & Face-Saving Actions: Track apology counts and face-saving or mitigating statements.
4. System Execution & Resource Metrics
These metrics establish technical and financial baselines for scaling up your experiments.
* Token Usage Statistics: Log the exact input and output token consumption per round and per episode on gpt-5-mini (or gpt-4o-mini) 13, 14.
* API Latency & Execution Cost: Measure the average time cost per turn and identify any bottlenecks in LLM API response times 13, 15.
* Action Correction Trigger Rate: Log how frequently TinyTroupe’s internal proposition and action-correction mechanism triggers to abort and regenerate out-of-character actions 16, 17.
